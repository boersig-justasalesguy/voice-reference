Five Strategic Technology Trends to Plan for in 2027

Every year, technology leaders face a long list of emerging technologies competing for attention and budget. The challenge is not finding trends. It is deciding which ones will change how the business competes over the next several years and which ones are noise. The five trends below are the ones we expect to have the greatest strategic impact in 2027.

1. Agentic AI moves into production workflows
AI agents that can plan multi-step tasks, call tools, and act on behalf of employees are moving from pilots into business processes such as customer service, IT operations, and finance. The planning question is no longer whether to use AI assistants but how to scope what agents can do, how their actions are logged, and where a human must approve. Organizations should set permission boundaries early and treat agents as a new class of identity with their own access controls.

2. AI governance becomes an operating function
As AI use spreads, organizations are formalizing who owns model risk, data use, and vendor due diligence. Governance will increasingly be built into procurement, security reviews, and product development rather than handled as a separate policy exercise. Leaders should name an accountable owner and connect governance to the systems teams already use.

3. Data products replace ad hoc data pipelines
Many organizations have accumulated thousands of pipelines with unclear ownership. A growing number are reorganizing data around managed products with defined owners, service levels, and documented schemas. This shift makes data more reliable for AI systems, which depend on trustworthy inputs. Planning should include a catalog of critical data assets and clear accountability for quality.

4. Security shifts toward resilience and recovery
Attack surfaces continue to grow across cloud, identity, and third-party software. Rather than trying to prevent every intrusion, leading organizations are investing in detection speed, containment, and recovery. Expect budgets to move toward incident response readiness, backup integrity, and automated containment that limits the blast radius of a compromise.

5. Sustainable computing becomes a design constraint
Energy costs, carbon reporting requirements, and the power demands of AI infrastructure are pushing organizations to measure the energy profile of their workloads. Efficiency will matter in architecture decisions, from model selection to where workloads run. Teams should begin tracking the compute and energy footprint of their most expensive services now.

How to use this list
Not every trend will apply to every organization. Start by mapping each trend to a business priority, identify one or two where the organization is already exposed, and assign a planning owner. The goal for 2027 is not to adopt everything but to be prepared for the changes most likely to affect your competitive position.
