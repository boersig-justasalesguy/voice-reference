### Decisions made

- **Quote limit:** at most one excerpt per source (Franklin, Gartner P2, Latio P1). Every other borrowed phrase is paraphrased.
- **Latio registers** are grouped by first-person rate counted in the raw text. P1–P3 run at 17–25 per 1,000 words, P4–P5 at 12.5 and 6.8, and P6–P9 at 0–2.
- **Author pronoun:** the Latio author is referred to neutrally ("the writer").
- **Hypothesis F**, that default AI prose reads like institutional writing, is not supported as stated:
  - Genre dominates.
  - AI analyst prose is narrowly closest to Gartner (4.44) over 2026 Latio (4.57), which is within noise.
  - Confirmed AI markers: triplets, contrast frames, placeholders, institutional "we", and missing named entities.
  - Not AI markers for these models: abstract verbs, hedging rate, and stock "tic" words.

### Known weaknesses still open at this checkpoint

- The vendor list used for M21 was partly drawn from Latio's own posts, which biases it.
- The baseline uses Claude models only, with one sample per prompt, generated by subagents rather than bare API calls.
- Forrester, Trollope, Twain and Latio's PDF reports are not covered.
- Aristotle, Cicero and Franklin–Strahan come from memory rather than primary sources.
- There is no real modern B2B email corpus. The "modern equivalent" lines are constructed examples.

## After checkpoint 1: evals (in progress, not merged into the ontology)

### Eval A: rewrite fidelity (`evals/A/`)

- **Setup:** the 15 AI B2B messages were rewritten by Sonnet using the ontology. Two blind judges (Opus and Haiku) compared each original with its rewrite.
- **Meaning:**
  - Both judges said the meaning was kept: Opus in 14 of 15 pairs, Haiku in 15 of 15.
  - No facts, numbers or placeholders were dropped, and nothing was added.
- **Strength drift:**
  - Both judges flagged the same 4 messages for drift: asks made harder, severity and a timeline softened, enthusiasm added.
  - Applying M19 ("we" → "I") changed who was doing the evaluation in one message.
  - In one message, "reported" became a stated fact.
- **"More human" preference:** not usable. Both judges favored whichever text was shown first (Text 1 vs Text 2: Opus 8–4, Haiku 10–2).
- **Planned fixes:**
  - Judge each pair twice, once in each order.
  - Add guards to the rewriter: never change how strong an ask or deadline is, never turn a report into an assertion, and swap "we" for "I" only when "we" is not a real team.

### Eval B: content differences (`evals/B/`)

- **Setup:** 34 texts, anonymized and cut to at most 700 words: AI analyst, AI practitioner, Gartner/IDC, and Latio early, transitional and late. Opus and Haiku code each text independently for claims, evidence, specificity, hedging and ownership.
- **Status:** done. Results are in `evals/B/results.json`; re-run with `evals/B/score.py`.

**Results** (ranges show the Haiku and Opus coders):

| Measure | AI practitioner | Latio early | AI analyst | Gartner/IDC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claims with any evidence | 0–2% | 23–31% | 16–17% | 29–40% |
| Claims with first-hand evidence | 0% | 18–20% | not compared | not compared |
| Prescriptive claims ("do X") | 35–37% | 0% | 19–26% | 8–14% |
| Evaluative claims (verdicts) | 23–27% | 50–66% | 13–19% | 18–30% |
| Named entities per text | 2.4–3.6 | 8–9.7 | 0.2–0.8 | 0.6–2.6 |
| Claims that are specific | 7–12% | 51–67% | not compared | not compared |
| Named criticisms per text | about 0 | 1.7–3.3 | not compared | not compared |
| Thesis an informed reader could dispute | 0.3–0.4 | 1.0 | not compared | not compared |

- **Reliability:**
  - Reliable (inter-coder r ≥ 0.75, same sign of difference): named entities 0.98, first-hand evidence 0.92, named criticism 0.90, prescriptive 0.84, evaluative 0.81, evidenced 0.76.
  - Not reliable (the coders disagree): claim count (Haiku extracts about a third as many claims; r = 0.38), hedging, named-example evidence, "no support", and author ownership.
- **Takeaway:** the AI texts give advice without verdicts. They tell the reader what to do, but they don't evaluate anything, rarely back a claim, and don't name or criticize anyone.
- **Caveats:**
  - **The AI writers were given no facts.** Part of the evidence gap comes from the setup, but it is also the realistic default for an unbriefed AI draft.
  - **Truncation:** all texts were cut to at most 700 words.
  - **Ownership not blinded:** the coders could guess ownership from register.
  - **T809 (opus_01) has placeholder statistics.** The Haiku coder counted them as cited data.
  - **Stake disclosure:** a statement of *no* stake was coded as not a disclosure. Under H03, that statement counts as the human pattern.

### Sales message eval (`evals/sales/`, done 2026-11-19)

**Source:** the user's "Sales Message Eval Set" artifact: 835 AI-generated messages with strong, average and weak labels plus buyer replies. The labels appear to have been generated along with the messages, so the results measure agreement with the labeller, not real buyer behavior. Full write-up: [evals/sales/REPORT.md](evals/sales/REPORT.md).

**Key results:**
- **Clichés are high-precision but low-recall.** When superlatives, "transform"-type wording, preambles or urgency appear, the message is labelled weak 73–92% of the time. Together these markers catch only 56% of weak messages.
- **Two markers reverse in sales.** M20 contrast frames appear 85% in strong messages, because they explain how the product works. M18 placeholders appear 68% in strong messages, mostly in scripts.
- **Content separates strong from weak, and the two coders agree.** The strong signals are buyer-centric sentences (0.93), industry pain (0.91–0.98), mechanism (0.84–0.86), value before the ask (0.84), and a small ask (0.77–0.82).
- **Rewrites do not change quality grades.** No stable preference holds once the order is counterbalanced. One ask got stronger. The judges caught a "would be" → "is" change in only 1 of 4 runs.

**Proposed ontology changes, not yet merged:**
- Narrow M20.
- Limit M18 to authenticity checks.
- Add content-level checks.
- Add a deterministic check for modal and hedge drift in rewrites.
