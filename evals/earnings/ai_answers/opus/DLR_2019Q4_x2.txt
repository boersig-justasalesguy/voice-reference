On roll-downs and churn, 2019 was our all-time high for lease expirations, and we came through it well. We signed over $500 million of renewals, retained a little over 80% of expiring leases, right in line with our long-term average, and cash rents rolled down 1.3%, which was much better than our initial expectation of down high single digits. Looking ahead, less than 16% of the portfolio expires in any given year, versus the 23% we faced at the start of last year, which should set the stage for a lower churn hurdle. We haven't seen broad-based rental rate growth in most markets yet, but the lion's share of the portfolio has recently been leased at current market rents, so we expect continued, gradual improvement in cash re-leasing spreads into 2020 and beyond, and we expect positive net absorption with improving occupancy in 2020.
